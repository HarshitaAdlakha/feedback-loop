Recommendation
Feedback Loop: a CV that reads in 7 seconds and explains itself in 30

Build one connected personal system in Canva instead of three loose pieces: a brand kit, a one-page visual CV, and a one-page Canva website that acts as the portfolio. The CV is the entry point; a QR code on it opens the site.

The twist that only Harshita can bring: each project is shown as a small causal loop (problem → what I did → what changed), the notation of system dynamics. Recruiters skim the top band; anyone curious follows the loop.

Why this one: it answers all three challenges with one story, it is grounded in real recruiter behaviour, and the loop motif is a visual identity nobody else in the room will have.

The problem

Recruiters skim; creative CVs often hide the facts

  • Ladders' eye-tracking study found recruiters spent about 6 seconds on an initial CV screen, revised to 7.4 seconds in 2018.
  • About 80% of that time went to six facts: name, current title and company, previous title and company, start and end dates, and education.
  • Other measurements differ (a 2023 tracking study put review time at 17–46 seconds), and Ladders sells CV services, so quote it as a range, not a law.

The design rule that follows: put those six facts where the eye lands first, and let the creative layer add depth without slowing the skim. Most "creative CVs" do the opposite.

The concept

Three reading depths, one design

7 s

The skim band

Top third of the CV. Name, one-line positioning, current and previous role, dates, education. Large, plain, scannable. No icons standing in for words.

30 s

The loops

Two or three project loops in the middle. Each is a ring with three nodes and a polarity sign: the problem, the intervention, the result. LineCheck goes first.

3 min

The website

QR code and short link to a one-page Canva site: the full LineCheck case study, the slides, the demo replay, and a contact block.

The motif

A project as a reinforcing loop

This is the visual unit to rebuild in Canva (Whiteboard or plain shapes, then reuse it as an element). Shown with LineCheck as the example.

On the CV, shrink each loop to three nodes plus one intervention arrow. Keep the + and − signs: they are the detail that tells judges this is real system dynamics, not decoration.

LineCheck, as a CV entry

LineCheck · Beta Fund × AWS × OpenAI Multi-Model Hackathon, San Francisco (remote) · Oct 2026
Co-built in one day with Paras Adlakha: an AI inspector aircraft technicians call on FaceTime. It inspects a photo of the part, cites the FAA repair manual, checks service history and airworthiness directives, and drafts the work order for a certificated mechanic to sign.
Loop: technician shortage → less experience per shift → more errors. LineCheck cuts the last link.

Prompt for Canva AI: monogram texture

Minimal abstract pattern of overlapping circular feedback loops with small arrowheads, flat vector, teal and warm amber on off-white, lots of empty space, no text

60-second pitch

Recruiters spend about seven seconds on a CV, and most of that on six facts. Creative CVs usually bury those facts under design. Mine is built in three depths. In seven seconds you get the facts. In thirty you get my projects as feedback loops, because I study system dynamics and that is how I think: a problem, what I changed, and what happened next. In three minutes, scan this QR code and you get the full story. The first loop is LineCheck, an AI aircraft inspector I helped build yesterday. Everything here was made in Canva today, on one brand kit, so the CV, the site and my LinkedIn all read as one system.
